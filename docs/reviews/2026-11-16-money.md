# CULT money review, 2026-11-16

Reviewer: money-reviewer agent. Scope: `src/` (Worker), `programs/` (hook, pool), `scripts/launch.mjs`. No source, program or script was changed. No transaction was sent on any cluster.

Tests added (each one FAILS on the code as reviewed and should pass once its finding is fixed):

- `test/review-money.test.js`, F1 to F10. `npm test` now reports 45 tests: the original 35 pass and the 10 review tests fail.
- `programs-tests/tests/review_hook.rs`: 3 reproductions that fail, plus 1 soundness check that passes (`review_pool_books_balance_over_many_trades`). Run with `cargo test --test review_hook` in WSL after `cargo build-sbf` for both programs. Both programs built cleanly with Solana 3.1.10, and the existing 4 LiteSVM tests pass.

Severity:

- **Blocker**: do not launch, or do not set `DRY_RUN="0"`, until it is fixed.
- **Before launch**: cheap to fix, and real money or a public claim depends on it.
- **After launch**: known limit or design choice. Fix later or disclose.

Within each band, findings that lose money come first, then wrong recipient, then stuck funds, then a wrong number shown.

## Ranked findings

### Blockers

**B1. The creator keeps the transfer-hook authority after launch: honeypot or fee bypass (lead 2)**

- **Where:** `scripts/launch.mjs:66` sets the hook authority to `creator.publicKey`. Step 5 (`:108`) revokes only the mint authority.
- **Scenario:** whoever holds the creator key calls Token-2022 `UpdateTransferHook`.
  - Set to `None`: the hook is gone. A Raydium or Meteora pool can then be opened, and sells there skip the 15%.
  - Pointed at a program that refuses transfers into the pool: buys still work, sells fail. That is a honeypot.
- **End state:** every holder's exit is controlled by one hot creator key, and the 15% has no enforcement.
- **Test:** `review_creator_cannot_switch_off_the_hook_after_launch`. The creator removes the hook, and another pool's vault then accepts $CULT.
- **Fix:** add `createSetAuthorityInstruction(mint, creator, AuthorityType.TransferHookProgramId, null, [], TOKEN_2022_PROGRAM_ID)` to step 5.
  - This does not break transfers. The mint keeps pointing at the hook program, and only the right to repoint it is removed.
  - The hook's code can still be fixed later through a program upgrade (see B2).
  - Prove it on devnet before mainnet: revoke, then one buy and one sell.

**B2. Program upgrade authorities contradict the site copy (lead 2)**

- **Where:** `public/app.js:82` says "Liquidity is locked and the tithe can never change". The README says "no admin, no withdraw".
- **Scenario:** while the deployer keeps upgrade authority over `paper-pool`, an upgrade can move all pool SOL (the pool PDA is owned by the program) and every vault token (the PDA signs). An upgrade of `paper-hook` can refuse sells.
- **End state:** "locked forever" is false until both programs are made final.
- **Fix:** decide one of these before launch, then make the copy match.
  - Finalize both now: `solana program set-upgrade-authority <id> --final`. The cost is that the hook bypasses (A1) can never be fixed, and the payout wallet can never be rotated (A5).
  - Move both authorities to a cold hardware key at launch, say so on the site, and finalize after the A1 hook fix is deployed and verified.
- **Recommended launch order:**
  1. Mint, hook list, fund the payout wallet (see L2), supply, pool, then revoke the mint and hook authorities. Steps 3 to 5 plus the hook revoke should be one transaction (see L1).
  2. Smoke test: one buy and one sell.
  3. Move the metadata pointer and update authorities to the cold key, or revoke them after the final rename.
  4. Handle the two program upgrade authorities as decided above.

**B3. Overlapping ticks pay the same batch twice (lead 1: confirmed and reachable)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:278-279` takes a 4-minute lock. `paying` (`:427-438`) sends from a list it read at the start, and its UPDATE to `'sent'` at `:436` has no condition.
- **Why it is reachable:**
  - Cloudflare allows a scheduled invocation 15 minutes of wall time ("Scheduled Workers have a maximum wall time of 15 minutes per invocation", Workers limits page).
  - One tick does a lot: `pollMint` (up to 50 signature pages and 300 `getTransaction` calls), snapshot, swap (up to 20 s of sleeps), allocate and paying.
  - `chain.rpc` backs off up to about 22 s per call when it gets 429s.
  - The site's `/api/trade`, `/api/send` and `/api/tx` use the same RPC key (see L4). On launch day, a tick lasting more than 4 minutes is plausible.
- **Scenario:**
  1. Tick A, slowed by throttling, starts sending batches.
  2. Its lock expires. Tick B takes the lock, reads the batches A hasn't reached as `'pending'`, and sends them.
  3. A continues from its stale list. It sends the same batches with its own blockhash and overwrites B's signature.
- **End state:** both copies land. Those holders are paid twice from the shared pot, and the batches sent last fail for lack of USDC. Those holders get nothing that round. D1 records only A's signature, so the books read as one payment.
  - The same race lets a stale snapshot step delete and rewrite `payouts` and move the drop back to `'swap'`.
  - It also lets two swaps be built before `swap_sig` is saved.
- **Test:** `F1` (12 of 20 holders sent their payout twice).
- **Fix:**
  - Claim a batch with `UPDATE batches SET status='sent', sig=?, lvbh=? WHERE drop_n=? AND i=? AND status='pending'`, and skip the batch if `meta.changes` is 0.
  - Re-arm with `... SET status=?, sig=NULL, tries=tries+1 WHERE drop_n=? AND i=? AND status='sent' AND sig=?`.
  - Lock TTL: raise to 15 minutes, the platform maximum.
  - Fence: before every step and every send, refresh the lock with `UPDATE kv SET at=<Date.now()+15m> WHERE k='lock' AND v=<token>`. Abort when `changes` is 0. Use `Date.now()`, not the tick's `now`.
  - Make `setDrop` status changes conditional on the expected old status.
- **Not reachable** while `DRY_RUN="1"`: dry-run batches are only marked as confirmed.

### Before launch

Every item here should be fixed before `DRY_RUN="0"`. Some are needed before launch itself, as marked.

**H1. Opening USDC accounts for new holders can be farmed out of the pot (loses money, leads 6 and 7)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:353` and `:374`. Snapshot reserves `missing.size * ATA_RENT` from the fee SOL before the swap. `chain.js:191` opens each new holder's USDC account at the house's cost.
- **Scenario:**
  1. An attacker moves $50 of CULT into a fresh wallet before each snapshot. Wallet-to-wallet moves are free.
  2. The house pays 0.00204 SOL (about $0.41) to open the wallet's USDC account.
  3. After the payout, the attacker moves the USDC out, closes the account (the rent goes back to its owner) and moves the CULT on to the next fresh wallet.
- **End state:** about $0.41 per fresh wallet per round moves from honest holders' pot to the attacker, at 144 rounds a day. It is capped only by the fee flow. Rent that exceeds the wallet's SOL also blocks the swap entirely.
- **Test:** `F4`. A whale receives 83.49 USDC against a fair share of 90.90, because 20 new wallets' rent came off the top.
- **Fix:** charge each new account's rent to that holder's own payout. Subtract the rent value in micro-USDC from `micro` for `needs_ata` rows, and drop rows that are left at or below the minimum. The alternative is to pay only wallets that already have a USDC account and roll the rest over.

**H2. One holder can block the 7 others in their batch (wrong recipient, not in the leads)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:345-349`. `needs_ata` is fixed at snapshot time. `chain.js:191-192`.
- **Scenario:** a holder with a USDC account is recorded as `needs_ata=0`, then closes that account before the payout. Their batch's transfer fails on all 5 tries and the batch is marked `'failed'`. A frozen USDC account (Circle freeze) has the same effect.
- **End state:** 7 honest holders get nothing that round, and their USDC rolls over to everyone, the griefer included. A large bag can choose to sit in batch 0 next to the biggest holders. Each round also costs 5 failed-transaction fees.
- **Test:** `F3` (7 holders unpaid).
- **Fix:** add `createAssociatedTokenAccountIdempotent` for every payee, not just `needs_ata` ones (a full batch of 8 already fits). Pair it with H1, or closing becomes a rent farm. After a batch's second failure, split it into single-wallet transactions to isolate frozen accounts.

**H3. A transaction that already landed is re-sent (lead 8: confirmed)**

- **Where:** `src/chain.js:147` maps `"processed"` to `null`. `src/indexer.js:414-415` reads statuses before block height. `:363` does the same for the swap.
- **Scenario:** a batch lands near its last valid block. At t1 its status reads `processed`, so the code sees `null`. At t2 the confirmed height already exceeds `lvbh`. The batch is re-armed and re-sent while the first transaction confirms.
- **Wider exposure:**
  - `rpc()` fails over to the next URL on 429, so the status and the height can come from different nodes.
  - A lagging node, or one with no transaction history, returns `null` for a transaction that landed.
- **End state:** a double payment, with the same fallout as B3.
- **Test:** `F2` (batch 0 re-sent: `tries` is 1 and the signature is new).
- **Fix:**
  - Treat any non-null status without an error as landed, and never re-send it.
  - Read the block height before the statuses.
  - Re-arm only when the height exceeds `lvbh + 150`, and confirm the absence with `getTransaction` on the primary provider.
  - Keep free and public endpoints out of the mainnet `SOLANA_RPC_URL` list.

**H4. One empty snapshot erases every holder's streak (lead 6, fails open)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:339` deletes every holder not seen in this snapshot. `:327-331` falls back to the D1 index without checking that it has caught up.
- **Scenario:** an RPC glitch returns no accounts, or DAS fails and the fallback index is stale or empty.
- **End state:** every row in `holders` is deleted. Every 7-day and 30-day streak restarts from zero at the next snapshot and cannot be recovered. This also happens during the `DRY_RUN` period.
- **Test:** `F6` (a 40-day holder's weight drops to 1.0x).
- **Fix:**
  - If `holders.size === 0`, or the count falls by more than half against the last snapshot, throw and retry next tick.
  - On the index fallback, require the same "caught up" check that index mode uses (`:323-324`).

**H5. A version-1 transaction stalls the history poller for good (not in the leads)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:195` uses `maxSupportedTransactionVersion: 0`. Mainnet has carried version-1 transactions since October 2026 (about 14% of a sample).
- **Scenario:** the first v1 transaction that touches the mint gets RPC error -32015. `pollMint` throws, and the cursor never moves again.
- **End state:**
  - Trades, the chart, volume and the holder index all stop.
  - If the Helius webhook isn't configured, sells stop being recorded, so streaks stop resetting.
  - The H4 fallback then reads a stale index.
- **Test:** `F9`.
- **Fix:** use `maxSupportedTransactionVersion: 1` here and at `test/devnet.live.mjs:29`, and alert when the poll cursor stops moving.

**H6. Unauthenticated endpoints spend the cron's RPC quota and relay arbitrary transactions (lead 5)**

- **Where:**
  - `src/trade.js:126-135`: `/api/send` relays any transaction that contains one pool instruction, extra instructions included.
  - `src/trade.js:137-141`: `/api/tx/:sig` makes one RPC call per request.
  - `/api/trade` makes two fresh RPC calls per request.
  - There is no rate limit, and the same key is used as the payout cron.
- **Scenario:** a spammer, or simply launch traffic, uses up the Helius quota. The tick then backs off on 429s, runs long, and makes B3 reachable.
- **End state:** throttled payouts, the B3 risk, and a free relay on the project's RPC key.
- **Test:** `F10` (relays a SOL transfer to an arbitrary address).
- **Fix:**
  - Give the cron its own RPC key that the site never uses.
  - Add a Cloudflare rate-limiting rule on `/api/send`, `/api/trade` and `/api/tx/*`.
  - In `/api/send`, accept only two compute-budget instructions plus one pool buy or sell.

**H7. A round usually pays without its own swap (lead 7: suspected provider behaviour, modelled in the test)**

- **Where:** `src/chain.js:130` and `:133`. `getBalance` and `getTokenAccountBalance` pass no commitment, so the RPC default is `finalized`. `allocate` runs seconds after the swap confirms, in the same tick.
- **End state:**
  - Round N's fees are paid to round N+1's holders. The first live round pays 0.
  - The amount the site shows as pending doesn't match what is paid.
  - No money is lost.
- **Test:** `F8` (0 of 30 USDC paid).
- **Fix:** pass `{ commitment: "confirmed" }` in both calls.

**H8. DRY_RUN rounds show on the site as USDC paid (wrong number on a public claim, lead 7)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:368` and `:432` store dry-run drops as `'done'` with `usdc_micro` set and batches as `'confirmed'`. `src/live.js:13`, `:34-37` and `liveWallet` then count them as paid.
- **Scenario:** the README's mainnet plan runs `DRY_RUN="1"` for a couple of hours.
- **End state:**
  - The site says "USDC airdropped" for payments that never happened.
  - At `DRY_RUN="0"`, the SOL saved during the dry run is paid to that moment's holders, so the site counts it twice.
- **Test:** `F5` (the site reports 20 USDC paid, with nothing sent).
- **Fix:** before setting `DRY_RUN="0"`, delete the dry-run rows from `drops`, `payouts` and `batches`. Alternatively, filter `swap_sig='dry-run'` and `sig LIKE 'dry-run-%'` out in `live.js`.

**H9. The payout wallet is never checked against the pool's on-chain payout (misconfiguration loses money, by reading, no test)**

- **Where:** `src/chain.js:55` checks only that the key matches `PAYOUT_WALLET`.
- **Scenario:** `PAYOUT_WALLET` and its key are set to any other wallet, for example one that already holds USDC.
- **End state:** the tick swaps that wallet's SOL above 0.05 and airdrops its entire USDC balance.
- **Fix:** at tick start, require `readPool(...).payout` to equal `cfg.payout`, or skip the tick.
- **Related guard:** in `weigh`, skip owners that are not on the curve. If `POOL_ACCOUNT` or `POOL_PROGRAM` were wrong, the vault would otherwise be paid almost the whole pot into a USDC account nobody can move.

### Launch-script and operations items (before launch)

**L1. `launch.mjs` sends five separate transactions with no resume (stuck state, by reading)**

- **Where:** `scripts/launch.mjs:37` generates the mint keypair and never saves it. `:64-108` send five separate transactions.
- **Scenario:** a confirmation timeout after step 3 or 4. This is common on mainnet even when the transaction landed.
- **End state:** the mint authority, and the hook authority, stay live. Re-running the script creates a second mint.
- **Front-run note:** anyone can create the hook's meta list first. It holds the same empty list, but the script's step 2 would then fail.
- **Fix:**
  - Send steps 3 to 5 plus the hook-authority revoke (B1) as one transaction: create the creator ATA, mint the supply, initialize the pool, revoke both authorities.
  - Write the mint address to disk before sending anything.

**L2. Small sells fail until the payout wallet holds SOL (stuck, lead 4)**

- **Where:** `programs/paper-pool/src/lib.rs:132` credits the fee directly to the payout wallet.
- **End state:** while that wallet holds 0 SOL, any sell whose fee is under the rent-exempt minimum (under about 0.006 SOL gross) fails with `InsufficientFundsForRent`.
- **Test:** `review_small_sell_works_before_payout_wallet_is_funded`.
- **Fix:** fund the payout wallet with 0.06 SOL (above the 0.05 SOL reserve) before the pool is initialized. Add this to the README's going-live steps.

**L3. The build created `target/deploy/paper_{pool,hook}-keypair.json`**

The timestamps match this review's `cargo build-sbf` run. I could not read their public keys (blocked). They are not the program ids. Deploy only with the keys in `program-keys/`.

### After launch

**A1. Fee-free routes around the hook (lead 3)**

The hook checks only the destination account's owner when a transfer happens (`programs/paper-hook/src/lib.rs:45-50`). The authority account at `:74-75` is never read.

- **(a) Reproduced.** A plain Token-2022 account (not an ATA) has no ImmutableOwner extension.
  - $CULT moves into it, and then `SetAuthority(AccountOwner)` hands it to anyone, an off-curve escrow included, with no transfer and no hook call.
  - An escrow program can run a market in token accounts with no 15%.
  - Test: `review_cult_refused_into_accounts_whose_owner_can_change`.
- **(b) Suspected, by reading.** A pool whose vault is owned by an on-curve wallet, with a program address approved as delegate for u64::MAX and the wallet's key thrown away.
  - Sells into it pass, because the destination owner is on the curve.
  - Buys out of it pass, because the buyer is on the curve.
  - That makes a trustless, fee-free AMM. Not reproduced: it needs a third program to sign as that address.
- **(c) By design.** Wallet-to-wallet transfers and delegate-style escrow OTC are untaxed. This is noted, not a bug.
- **Fix in the hook:**
  - Refuse destinations without the ImmutableOwner extension. ATAs, including the pool vault, always have it.
  - Refuse when the transfer authority is off the curve and is not the pool address.
  - Deploy this before finalizing the hook program (B2).

**A2. Streak gaming (lead 6: confirmed)**

- **Where:** `src/indexer.js:60` and `:335-338`. `since` starts when a wallet is first seen holding anything, and does not move when the balance grows.
- **Scenario:** a dust wallet aged 30 days receives a big bag (bought, or moved in wallet-to-wallet with no tax) and gets 1.5x on all of it. Exits through a second wallet never reset the aged one.
- **End state:** the "devotion" multiplier rewards gaming, and the pot shifts toward gamers.
- **Test:** `F7` (weight 15M on a 10M bag bought the day before).
- **Fix (design decision for Kay):** keep a `base` column holding the lowest balance seen since `since`. Weight is then `base * mult + (balance - base) * 1`.

**A3. Other after-launch items**

- **Swap transaction signed blind.** The Jupiter transaction is signed without inspection (`chain.js:175-176`). A compromised or spoofed Jupiter response could drain the hot wallet, bounded by about one round of fees. Fix: allow-list program ids, or simulate it and check balance changes.
- **Fee budget.** `TX_FEE` is 15,000 lamports. A full batch actually costs 5,000 + 266,000 CU × 0.05 = 18,300 lamports. The swap's `auto` priority fee and the payout wallet's own USDC account rent are not budgeted. This heals itself, because `spare` is recomputed from the real balance after the reserve each round. Many new holders without USDC accounts delay swaps but do not stall them permanently.
- **DAS paging may double-count (suspected).** A transfer between pages that are read sequentially could count the same tokens twice. Helius's cursor ordering is unverified. It doesn't matter while there are fewer than 1000 holders.
- **The pool's payout address is permanent (A5).** If `PAYOUT_SECRET_KEY` leaks, every future fee goes to the thief, and once the pool program is final there is no remedy. Weigh this into B2.

## Leads from the brief

1. **Tick lock and double-pay:** confirmed and reachable. See B3. The proposed minimal fix (conditional UPDATE plus lock refresh) is right, but it also needs the conditional re-arm, a 15-minute TTL, and `Date.now()` in the refresh.
2. **Authorities after launch:** confirmed. See B1 and B2.
   - Revoking the hook authority breaks nothing, and the hook program can still be upgraded.
   - Freeze authority is null at creation.
   - The metadata pointer and update authorities remain with the creator. They are cosmetic, but scanners flag them.
   - No PermanentDelegate, TransferFee or ConfidentialTransfer extensions are present.
3. **Hook bypass:** confirmed. See A1.
   - The SetAuthority(AccountOwner) route works, but through a market in token accounts, not by opening a second pool.
   - An account whose owner moves to a program address can't receive sells.
   - The delegate route is suspected.
   - CPI calls still pay the fee.
   - Plain Transfer is refused for hook mints.
   - OTC is untaxed by design.
4. **Pool math:** sound. See "Checked and sound". The one gap is L2 (unfunded payout wallet).
5. **`/api/send`:** confirmed. See H6.
6. **Snapshot gaming:**
   - Streak gaming confirmed (A2).
   - Empty-snapshot fail-open is new (H4).
   - DAS paging double count suspected.
   - MIN_HOLD_USD price manipulation refuted as an exploit. Weights are in tokens, and price only gates eligibility. Pushing the price down to exclude others costs the 15% sell fee. A free buy that pushes it up only lets more wallets in.
   - Rent farming is new (H1).
7. **Whole-balance allocation:** fine by design. USDC sent to the payout wallet is redistributed.
   - The previous drop can't have batches in flight, because `done` requires none pending or sent. That holds unless B3 strikes.
   - The real risks are H9 (misconfiguration), H2 (failed batches roll over), and H7 (the swap read one round late).
   - The fee reserve heals itself. See A3.
8. **sigStatuses on failover:** confirmed. See H3.

## Checked and sound

- **Crash windows in a single tick.** The signature is saved before the send, and a resend happens only after the blockhash expires. This applies to `paying` and to the swap. The existing `drop.test.js` covers it.
- **Sells seen by both the webhook and the poller.** Deduplicated by `INSERT OR IGNORE` on the signature. `sellers` is updated only on a new insert. The webhook secret is checked.
- **Lock release** matches the lock's token, so a tick can only release its own lock.
- **`allocate`.** The sum never exceeds the pot, and amounts under the minimum roll over. Floating-point error is about 1e-4 micro-USDC, far below 1.
- **Pool program:**
  - Buy and sell round toward the pool. The fee rounds down by less than 1 lamport, in the seller's favour.
  - Math is checked u128, with `overflow-checks = true` in the release profile.
  - `gross` is always less than `sol_reserve`, and the pool stays rent-exempt.
  - Accounts are pinned: pool seeds, `has_one` for the mint, vault and payout, the user ATA, and a token program matched to the mint's owner.
  - No reentrancy.
  - Donating tokens or SOL to the pool can't be turned into a profit.
  - `review_pool_books_balance_over_many_trades` runs 80 random trades: lamports always equal rent plus the SOL reserve, x*y never shrinks, and the payout wallet receives exactly the sum of fees.
- **Pool initialization can't be front-run.** It needs tokens of the mint, and the whole supply only ever sits with the creator.
- **`/api/trade`** derives every address on the server. Reserves sent by the client only set the caller's own `min_out`.
